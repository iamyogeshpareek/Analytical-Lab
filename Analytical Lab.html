1. Upload and inspect data

Upload the raw concentration and Area/Absorbance data only. The app scans the workbook for recognized headers and calculates the derived results locally in your browser.
Help · accepted Excel / CSV layout and column names

Use one header row per table, one observation per row, numeric cells only in the data area, and no merged cells. Include measurement units in the concentration header when useful. Keep raw readings separate; omit averages, SD, %RSD and predicted columns.

Linearity (single reading allowed)

Concentration | Area or Conc | Abs

Triplicates: Concentration | Area 1 | Area 2 | Area 3. Headers such as Concentration / Conc and Absorbance / Abs are recognized. Replace Area with Absorbance, Abs, Intensity, Signal, Response, Replicate, or Reading. A single Mean/Average column can be used for linearity only; it cannot provide replicate precision. At least 3 distinct levels are needed to calculate; ICH Q2(R2) recommends at least 5 appropriately distributed levels for linearity evaluation.

Accuracy / recovery

Concentration (µg/mL) | Absorbance 1 | Absorbance 2 | Absorbance 3

Use nominal/spiked concentration and three raw responses per level. In Excel, put the table on a sheet named Accuracy or Recovery. Choose the matching calibration set when prompted.

Precision

Sample | Result 1 | Result 2 | Result 3

For raw instrument responses use a recognized response name with 1–3 and select the calibration to convert to concentration. For already calculated reportable results, use Result 1–3 and select “Already measured result.” For day/run groups, add consecutive triplicate groups (1–3, 4–6, etc.) and confirm only if documented.

LOD / LOQ

Concentration | Response 1

The selected calibration table supplies slope and residual SD. Default: residual SD of the unweighted regression on level means, with df = number of levels − 2. You may select residual SD from all individual readings for replicate input. The estimate is calculated separately for each detected calibration table. Do not combine different analytes or procedures. LOD = 3.3σ/|slope|; LOQ = 10σ/|slope|. The resulting concentration units follow the concentration column. Confirm the estimated lower limit with suitable low-level accuracy/precision data.

Robustness

Sample | Flow rate | Concentration | Area 1 | Area 2 | Area 3

Use factor/condition columns (for example, flow rate or pH), at least two settings, and repeat the same anonymous sample/concentration at each setting. Without factor labels and matching conditions, the tool will mark robustness as not calculated.

Accepted column names

Concentration / Conc

Header detection ignores title rows and accepts concentration units in parentheses. Single response names: Area, Absorbance, Abs, Mean, Average, Intensity, Signal, or Response. Triplicate headers can be numbered with or without spaces, such as Area 1–3, Abs1–3, Absorbance 1–3, or Response 1–3. Data cells must contain numeric raw readings; calculated summaries are not required. CSV files must be comma-separated.

Example values above are illustrative only. Never include sample identifiers or confidential details unless needed in your own local workbook; workbook content is not included in the generated report.

Accuracy calibration mapping

Accuracy blocks default to calibration sets in the same order when their counts match. Check or change the analyte-to-curve mapping if your workbook uses a different order.

Precision result mapping

For raw response readings, select the matching analyte calibration set. Precision %RSD is calculated on the back-calculated concentration. Choose “Already measured result” only when the uploaded values are already reportable result values.

Optional acceptance criteria from your protocol

Leave limits blank when your protocol does not specify them. Recovery limits apply to mean recovery at each nominal level.

Intra-day, inter-day, and robustness %RSD criteria are entered separately. The general %RSD limit is a fallback for precision and robustness. The 1% and 2% options are convenience presets, not universal ICH acceptance limits.
